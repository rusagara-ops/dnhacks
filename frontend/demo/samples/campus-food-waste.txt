Campus Food Waste Reduction Trial

The fictional Northbridge College dining service completed a six-week food-waste trial in its East Dining Hall. The trial covered weekday lunches only. Other dining halls and evening meals were not included.

Before the trial, staff measured an average of 120 kilograms of discarded food per week. During the final two weeks of the trial, the average was 84 kilograms per week, a 30 percent reduction. Attendance was approximately stable, but the team did not run a controlled experiment, so it cannot attribute the entire reduction to any single change.

The team introduced smaller default portions, offered free second servings, and placed signs near the serving stations encouraging students to take only what they expected to eat. Students could still request a larger first portion. The trial did not remove any menu options.

Dining manager Luis Patel coordinated the trial with student volunteer Amina Yusuf. The project cost $2,400 for weighing equipment, signs, and staff training. Staff estimated that reduced ingredient purchasing saved $900 during the six weeks. This estimate excludes staff time and does not mean the project has recovered its initial cost.

Of 160 students who answered a voluntary survey, 118 supported keeping the smaller default portions. Respondents were self-selected, so the survey may not represent the whole student population. Several students requested clearer signs explaining that second servings were free.

The team recommends extending the trial for one semester in East Dining Hall, improving the signs, and measuring waste per meal served. Expansion to other dining halls has been proposed but has not been approved. The facilities committee will review the proposal in October.
